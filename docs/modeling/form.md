# Groom, body and clothing

## Hair

Establish the cut on a scalp, sculpt or groom first. Study crown height, part, hairline, silhouette, major clumps and where they expose the face from all available sides. Curves and card profiles carry direction and cross-section. A cap carries coverage beneath the cards. Many short, layered ends can produce a different cut from long root-to-tip ribbons even with the same outline.

Keep an editable groom and test the actual edit loop. Select a lock, save an override, regenerate, reopen, and verify its neighbors. Procedural scatter can renumber every root when one row changes. Save roots or other stable identifiers when local art direction matters. A seeded color per card or guide makes a screenshot actionable. Inspect a full-size crop when a crest looks toothed or ends look blunt. Cards emerging through an upper layer can resemble a bad taper. Trace which card is visible before changing the tip.

Export the groom in the target engine's representation and test alpha coverage, direction of highlights, sorting and the angle where the cap becomes visible. Move the light and rotate the head. Check the reflection or other passes that the player actually sees. Reuse a prebuilt hairstyle when it already carries the desired cut and the source allows the next requested edit. [Hair Tool overview](https://joseconseco.github.io/HairTool_3_Documentation/overview)

## Body and garment

Start with the body's structural proportions and the garment's construction. Identify which silhouette belongs to skin, cloth ease, compressed fabric, a lapel or a belt. Set the shoulder, elbow, wrist and waist on the posed figure. Check the front, side and back. A useful body source preserves the existing skeleton or has a tested transfer to it. Consider hands, exposed neck and feet at the same handoff.

For sewn clothing, keep the flat pattern, seam correspondence, grain direction, fabric and rest pose. Change a sleeve opening on its pattern and simulate again. Check whether mirrored panels share a curve before making an asymmetric edit. Redraping can change vertex order. Carry weights by correspondence and test the actual motion, including return from full reach. When using an authored, fitted mesh, preserve the maker's cut and original folds as a comparison. A seam that needs to turn heavy cloth often needs a supporting cut and cloth volume. A painted line tests its placement only. [Garment Tool pattern operations](https://joseconseco.github.io/GarmentToolDocs/pattern_tools/) · [Weight transfer via inpainting](https://github.com/rin-23/RobustSkinWeightsTransferCode)

Judge belts and folds at torso size as well as in detail. Separate belt path, cross-section and knot construction. Show the same motion under garment material and plain light to distinguish a deformation fault from a baked fold. Record where any pose corrective starts, peaks and releases. Geometric clearance and screen-space silhouette answer different questions.
