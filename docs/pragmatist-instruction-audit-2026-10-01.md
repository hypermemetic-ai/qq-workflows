# Pragmatist audit of agent instructions — 2026-10-01

Our instruction stack contains useful domain knowledge, but repeatedly converts temporary circumstances into durable prohibitions and substitutes procedural completion for judgment. The strongest demonstrated failure remains ISO's unchanged local studio after publication. The broader audit also found duplicated delivery guidance, compulsory orientation reading, permission boundaries attached to methods, and supplier workflows that prescribe incidental choices.

The remedy is primarily subtraction and relocation. Keep the knowledge an intelligent colleague would need; remove the accumulated management of that colleague. OpenSpec's stock planning skills remain valuable and unchanged.

## What pragmatism means here

The operator's preference concerns how agents know and judge, as well as whether they act. A useful starting point is the concrete situation and the purpose the user is pursuing. The agent's formulation of the problem, chosen means, plans and assumptions are provisional. Inquiry should address uncertainty that could change a decision. Acting provides evidence; its consequences can correct the solution and the understanding of the problem itself.

This supports initiative, proportionate investigation and revisable judgment. It also supports pausing when a consequential uncertainty warrants it. Speed, obedience, activity and a passing check are each inadequate as universal measures of success. The question is what the available evidence establishes about the purpose being served.

Applied to ISO, a release hash establishes which source was published. It does not establish that the operator's Source control works, that the local comparison uses the intended edition, or that the geometry makes musical relationships intelligible. Those are different claims requiring relevant observations. A narrower child change can be sound while the broader request remains open.

## Findings and concrete edits

### 1. Temporary restrictions are being promoted into policy

Ten project guides carry the same readiness-only no-deployment/shared-runtime/second-primary boilerplate. Thirteen readiness maps repeat that migration changes remain intentionally uncommitted. ISO's guide embeds a cancelled-task snapshot, requires the old checkpoint before any implementation, and ends with an unconditional live5175 no-restart/no-switch restriction. Music's readiness map still narrates the conversation as awaiting a disk-selection photo. These were useful migration facts; startup routing makes them recurring decision inputs after the assignment changes.

**Remove** completed readiness/smoke restrictions from evergreen guides. **Relocate** snapshots and receipts to dated migration/task records. Preserve still-live drain conditions until they are actually satisfied. Keep useful source maps discoverable without making every task reread the migration. The current task and observed state determine applicability; an old pause does not establish the authority of a later assignment.

### 2. Our own delivery repair accumulates instructions

`docs/codex-global-AGENTS.md` repeats persistence, approval and reporting directions already present in the native base. `qq-workflows/AGENTS.md` repeats the delivery rule and prescribes the same Git check at fixed task boundaries. Voice's current guide additionally contains the entire shared guide, including the `--- project-doc ---` delimiter, above its own project content. This is a concrete example of copying a composed instruction view into a source guide.

**Remove** shared-guide copies from project guides. **Condense** the shared source to the operator's actual preferences: ordinary scoped Git delivery is included in repository work; zero unattended Git work is the aim. Keep the Git-status helper available as the inexpensive source of fresh upstream/local state. Repeated instructions to be persistent, obtain fewer approvals and use a particular report format add little beyond the stock base. The fixed-boundary check is a candidate for simplification, not a reason to lose Git awareness.

### 3. Startup reading becomes a substitute for selecting evidence

Several guides require README, readiness, handoff and capability reading at entry regardless of the task. DecIQ Logic publishing adds a broad prerequisite bundle; some of that bundle matters for executable logic, while less is needed for a wording correction.

**Replace** owned undifferentiated reading assignments with concise source locations and the circumstances in which their facts matter. Preserve direct warnings about scripts with side effects.

**Correction after operator clarification:** Gemini's two identical 2,935-byte search guides are developer-maintained ZG guidance, not locally authored workflow instructions. The earlier recommendation to trim their retrieval routing is withdrawn. Preserve ZG's indexing strategy and maintained guidance; its presence in a Gemini directory does not make it an owned harness dependency.

### 4. Permissions are attached to methods instead of consequences

The restart contract requires a fresh confirmation even when the user has requested immediate poweroff, and operator verification that tasks may pause before every reboot. It also promises to snapshot “all 9” Orca sessions and describes a fixed historical topology as invariant. ISO score research repeatedly requires separately authorized provisioning, conversion or correction; that is appropriate for a research worker, but can create a second boundary when the primary already owns an authorized comparison outcome.

**Retain** current-machine identity, physical power effects, unsaved-work protection, rights and account boundaries. **Remove** redundant confirmation where the existing request already establishes the action and its consequences. **Move** historical topology/counts into discoverable operational facts and check actual state when consequential. In research guidance, make the research-only boundary apply to a research-only assignment; preserve rights/access decisions that really require the operator. The ISO guide currently explicitly preserves that skill unchanged, so this is a proposed source correction, not an installed edit.

### 5. Evidence vocabulary can dominate the work

ISO requires visual-impact labels and merged/served/accepted distinctions, while also prohibiting screenshots and generated review images alongside substitute renderers. Legacy tooling tracks revisions, acknowledgments, PASS/FAIL and completion markers. These distinctions can be useful, but the observed ISO report gave publication evidence and preserved-state assurances while the intended local behavior was absent. More mandatory final-report fields would strengthen the same failure mode.

**Remove** compulsory report boilerplate and omitted-action accomplishment language from owned templates. Keep receipts as traceable supporting evidence. Preserve the real-engine studio as the relevant musical comparison surface; reconsider an observation ban where an authentic capture would help inspect that surface. A mock renderer and a capture of the actual renderer have different evidential value. Select observations for the claim being made, with proportionate effort, rather than constructing a universal completion gate.

### 6. Legitimate constraints are mixed with generic caution

Privacy, client-controlled release, musical source fidelity, physical disk identity and saved work have concrete consequences. DecIQ Logic's human merge/release contract is deliberate; the global normal-delivery preference does not remove it. Inbox's staged provider-mutation boundary protects real messages. Windows-desktop guidance explains that an action can succeed before screenshot transfer fails, so observing before retrying prevents duplicate input. These are useful reasons for a capable agent to judge well.

**Retain** those facts and boundaries, preferably once at their owning source. Separate them from “no runtime changes” or “leave uncommitted” language inherited from documentation assignments. The current native Windows skill's wrong-target and partial-input explanations are especially worth preserving. Its Pi copy differs; blindly synchronizing installed copies would erase deployment-specific knowledge.

### 7. Supplier guidance also contains incidental prescriptions

The native base already favors completion and continuity of authorization. Its extensive writing prohibitions and interaction micro-rules can nevertheless turn communication into another compliance task. Among conditional skills, Sites says generally to use three discovery questions when even one is meaningful, prescribes image counts and forbids arrows on buttons. Pages restricts heading punctuation. Image generation carries an extensive taxonomy and an 18-step workflow. These are candidates for supplier simplification; they are not evidence that those bodies caused ISO's native failure.

OpenAI Docs requires official-documentation search before local inspection even for local troubleshooting; the higher-priority web tool instructions require local inspection first for OpenAI usage questions. That is an actual ordering conflict. Conversely, supplier skill-creator guidance explicitly assumes Codex is capable, removes generic/repeated advice, and reserves fixed sequences for fragile or consequential work. Sites hosting already publishes by default without a redundant conversational deployment confirmation. The supplier stack is not uniformly conservative.

**Keep** stock packages and concrete API/file-format contracts. Address supplier defects upstream or through supported selection, rather than maintaining patched caches or overriding the stock base with a larger local prompt. Conditional skill descriptions should route by the actual task; their presence alone should not trigger a workflow.

### 8. Retiring prompts are a separate layer

`workflow/architect-profile.mjs` replaces Pi's stock base with the owned Architect prompt and selected project context. `workflow/worker-config.mjs` supplies custom worker instruction files. Managed Pi roles further restrict tests, edits and Git; communication appends exact acknowledgment/completion mechanics. Some role text supports sound judgment, but the system as a whole distributes authority across tickets, roles and transport states.

**Retire** these instruction paths with the already approved migration after the remaining host and owned consumers drain. Do not port their administrative machinery into native Codex or spend effort polishing role prompts that native primaries do not load. Preserve the independent products/helpers and their factual contracts.

## Proposed minimal shared direction

If one epistemic preference is retained after subtraction, this is the candidate:

> Exercise judgment in pursuit of the user's purpose. Treat plans, rules and assumptions as revisable tools: understand what they are for and whether they apply here. Investigate uncertainty that matters, act when you know enough, and let observed consequences improve your understanding of both the problem and your next move.

This is a proposed replacement for accumulated generic guidance, not an extra layer to append. Keep the short, concrete Git-delivery preference beside it if needed. Project guides then supply project knowledge and real boundaries. Dated task records carry temporary authority and state. Stock OpenSpec supplies the requested planning operation.

The first cleanup should remove the demonstrated local freeze and stale readiness clauses, eliminate copied shared guidance, and make startup references conditional. Subsequently shorten owned skills where permission or method rules outrun their purpose. Follow the existing retirement program for legacy prompt paths. Supplier changes remain a separate responsibility. The Workflows primary owns coordination; independent project primaries own corrections to their current tasks.

Judge that cleanup in ordinary real work: does the agent resolve relevant uncertainty, adapt when evidence changes, and carry the user's purpose through delivery with less avoidable ceremony? Use the next relevant tasks as evidence. A new audit framework, mandatory scorecard or instruction to audit instructions on every task would recreate the problem.
